About

A polka show at 16, weddings ever since.

I got my first DJ gig at 16 at a radio station in St. Marys, PA, hosting the Sunday morning polka show and filling in on the pop shows when they'd let me.

I went to Point Park for broadcast journalism, kept DJing house parties and then bar gigs, and somehow ended up with an IT degree and a corporate job in 2009. The bar gigs never stopped. Around 2012 some wedding DJ friends showed me how to bring what I did in bars to weddings, and in 2014 I quit the day job to do this full time. 400+ weddings later, I still think I got the better end of that deal.

I'm a mixing DJ, which means every song blends into the next with no dead air. It doesn't mean I only play club music. It means the energy on the floor never drops between songs. My favorites, if you're asking, are 90s and early 2000s pop and hip-hop, 70s and 80s funk and disco, and 90s and 2000s rock and pop punk. Mid-2000s hip-hop and dance music never get old to me either.

Anyone with speakers and a laptop can call themselves a DJ. I'm a student of this. I'm comfortable in a lot of genres and decades, I build a custom soundtrack for every event, and I read the crowd all night to figure out what to play next. I work closely with every other vendor in the room, I know my gear inside out, and I have a backup for everything. And sometimes I have a handlebar mustache, which is always a hit.

Questions people ask

The FAQ

How would you describe your DJ style?
I'm a club or mixing-style DJ, which doesn't mean I only play club music. It means I blend each song into the next with no gaps of dead air. That keeps a constant flow of energy on the dance floor and keeps your guests engaged.
How many weddings have you DJed?
More than 400 since 2012, averaging 30 to 40 a year.
Can we come see you perform?
Absolutely. Message me and I'll tell you where I'll be.
I've never planned a wedding. Can you help?
Of course. I'm always available by call, text, email or video, and planning meetings are unlimited. The planning app has song suggestions for every part of the night if you need inspiration, and the Planning page has everything I tell couples in our meetings.
Do you act as the emcee?
Yes. I make the announcements that need making and try not to be overly talkative.
How do you define your emcee style?
It depends on the event. Some call for a high-energy sports style and others for something calmer. Unless you tell me otherwise, I use my best judgment based on you and your guests.
What do you do if no one is dancing?
First thing, change the song as fast as possible. Then I might switch to a completely different genre. If that doesn't work, a slow song or a group dance, unless you've told me not to. The best way to get people dancing at a wedding is to get the newlyweds on the floor. Everyone follows.
Will you teach the guests the Cupid Shuffle?
No. I'll play it though, and hopefully everyone catches on.
Do you get out on the floor and dance with guests?
No.
Do you say a bunch of cheesy stuff on the microphone?
No.
Do you take requests from guests?
If it fits the vibe, absolutely. There's a setting in the planning app where you tell me whether to.
Do you play clean music?
Unless you tell me otherwise, everything is a radio edit. Most profanity and some subject matter are removed, and everything would be fine on public radio.
Do you DJ multicultural weddings?
I don't bill myself as a multicultural DJ, but I'm comfortable playing whatever my clients ask for. I ask for the specific songs ahead of time and study them before the wedding. I love researching traditions and learning new music.
How do you keep your music library current?
I subscribe to several record pools for professional DJs, which have the classics and the latest releases. What I can't find there, I buy. I don't download from YouTube or anywhere like it, because I don't settle for anything less than the best audio quality.
How far will you travel?
Anywhere. I've DJed weddings in Arizona, Florida, New York, Virginia, Maryland, Ohio, Tennessee, West Virginia and all over Pennsylvania. More than 50 miles from Pittsburgh adds a travel fee, and if there's a flight involved, equipment rental may be needed.
How far in advance should we book?
Popular Saturdays from April through November book about a year out.
How do we secure our date?
A signed contract and a deposit, usually about a third of the total.
When is the final payment due?
Two weeks to a month before the wedding. I like to settle it before the day so I don't have to bother you when things get hectic. Cash, check, Zelle, Venmo, PayPal, CashApp or card.
How early do you arrive?
For weddings, two to three hours before guests are scheduled. I'm usually loaded in, set up and sound checked within one to two hours depending on what you've booked.
What do you need from us on the day?
As long as space allows, I bring my own DJ booth, so nothing. A spot near power.
Do you require a hot meal?
I tell people the reason I got into DJing is the food. A hot meal is always appreciated, never required.
What if the party's still going at the end time?
I don't book anything else on your day, so I'm always willing to stay. That's why reception coverage is unlimited hours. No overtime.
Do you offer discounts?
Yes. Non-Saturday dates April through November and any off-season date are discounted, and I offer a discount to active military and first responders as a thank you for your service.

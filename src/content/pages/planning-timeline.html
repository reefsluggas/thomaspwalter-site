How to build a reception timeline that doesn't feel rushed

A reception that feels relaxed has the formalities done inside the first 90 minutes after introductions, dinner served promptly, and the dance floor open with at least two and a half hours to go. Most timelines go wrong by putting too much between dinner and dancing.

A timeline that works

[Draft body. The direct answer above is the part that matters for search; the rest gets written from your whiteboard walkthrough. Rough shape:]

  • Cocktail hour is 60 minutes, not 90. By minute 70 people are checking their phones.
  • Introductions go straight into the first dance while everyone's already looking at you.
  • Toasts happen during dinner, between courses, not after it.
  • Cake cutting and parent dances go back to back, so the floor opens once and stays open.
  • Two and a half hours of dancing is the minimum. Three is better.

A sample Saturday

5:00 ceremony · 5:30 cocktail hour · 6:30 introductions and first dance · 6:40 welcome and blessing · 6:45 dinner, toasts between courses · 7:45 cake, parent dances · 8:00 open dancing · 10:55 last song · 11:00 send-off

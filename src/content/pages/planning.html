Planning resources

Planning tips from 400+ weddings

I've watched more than 400 weddings happen from the best seat in the house. These are the things I tell every couple in our planning meetings, written down so you can read them at 11pm on your phone. Nothing here is a sales pitch. If something's useful, use it. If you want to talk any of it through, my calendar's open.

Just booked

How to build a reception timeline that doesn't feel rushedA reception that feels relaxed has the formalities done inside the first 90 minutes after introductions, dinner served promptly, and the dance floor open with at least two and a half hours to go.
→
Ceremony music: prelude, processional and recessional explainedYou need three kinds of ceremony music: 20 to 30 minutes of prelude while guests are seated, one to three processional songs for the walk in, and one recessional song for the walk out.
→
Must play, do not play, and why the second list matters moreGive me 15 to 25 must plays, not 60, and spend real time on the do not play list. The do not play list is what keeps your night from sounding like someone else's wedding.
→

Six months out

Cocktail hour music sets the tone for the whole nightCocktail hour music should be heard, not listened to. Pick a mood rather than individual songs, keep it at talking volume, and let it hint at where the night is going.
→
Grand entrance: the song, the order and the namesOne song for the wedding party and one for the two of you, the names in order with pronunciations, and a decision on pairs or one at a time. Under four minutes.
→
First dance and parent dances: picking the song and the editPick the song that means something, then let me edit it. A first dance should be two to two and a half minutes.
→

Three months out

Toasts: mic handling and keeping them on timeTwo to three toasts, during dinner, two to four minutes each, handheld wireless mic, in an order you decide ahead of time.
→
Should we take guest requests?Yes, with a filter. Turn on the guest request link in the planning app, tell me whether to take requests on the night, and trust me to play the ones that fit.
→
How I get people on the dance floorThe two of you get on the dance floor first, and everyone follows. After that it's reading the room. Dead air is the enemy.
→
Multicultural and family music: how to prep meSend me the specific songs and the traditions early, with the names of the dances and who leads them. I'll learn them before the wedding.
→

Final month

Last song: ending the night on purposeA big last song with everyone on the floor, a private last dance after the send-off, or both. Then tell me the song.
→
What your venue and vendors need from the DJA spot near power, roughly six by eight feet, and about two to three hours before guests arrive. Your photographer and planner need my number. That's the whole list.
